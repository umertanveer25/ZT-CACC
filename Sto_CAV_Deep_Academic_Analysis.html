Executive Technical Summary (One-Sentence Synthesis)

Sto-CAV introduces a modular, geographically distributed co-simulation platform that bridges microscopic road traffic simulators (SUMO), multi-RAT communications (ITS-G5, LTE-V2X, Optical VLC), and physical Hardware/Devices-in-the-Loop (DIL) across distributed edge servers via a centralized Control Unit (CU), achieving millisecond-accurate latency benchmarking for safety-critical Connected and Automated Vehicle (CAV) applications.

1. Research Background & Motivation

Connected and Automated Vehicles (CAVs) represent the cornerstone of future Cooperative Intelligent Transport Systems (C-ITS). To achieve safe autonomous driving (SAE Level 4/5), cooperative maneuvering, and high-density platooning, vehicles must exchange time-critical situational awareness data (such as ETSI CAM, DENM, and 3GPP BSM packets) with sub-100ms or even sub-10ms latency bounds. However, physical real-world road trials involving dozens of automated vehicles are logistically intractable, economically prohibitive, and hazardous under emergency scenarios.

Consequently, researchers rely heavily on computer simulations. Existing vehicular network simulation frameworks (such as Veins, Artery, PLEXE, and Simu5G) have propelled the field forward, but they suffer from significant systemic bottlenecks when faced with next-generation vehicular ecosystems:

  • Single-RAT Homogeneity: Most frameworks model only a single radio access technology at a time (e.g., standard IEEE 802.11p/ITS-G5 or single-cell LTE-V2X), failing to capture multi-RAT hybrid access where vehicles dynamically switch between RF and Optical Wireless Communications (OWC/VLC).
  • Lack of Real Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL/DIL) Integration: Software-only simulations rely on idealized physical-layer curves, ignoring physical transceiver queuing delays, operating system jitter, firmware overheads, and real hardware channel impairments.
  • Monolithic Compute Bottlenecks: Running complex vehicle kinematics, ray-tracing optical channels, and large-scale RF packet scheduling on a single workstation leads to severe simulation deceleration and temporal desynchronization.

To overcome these foundational limitations, Masini et al. propose Sto-CAV (Simulation Platform for Connected and Automated Vehicles), a distributed, modular framework enabling heterogeneous communication technologies and physical devices located in disparate geographical laboratories to co-execute in real time.

3. Mathematical Formulation of Sto-CAV Heterogeneous Communications

Sto-CAV formally characterizes packet transmission and reception across heterogeneous radio frequency (RF) and optical wireless (OWC/VLC) channels.

A. RF Channel & Signal-to-Interference-plus-Noise Ratio (SINR)

For RF links (ITS-G5 and LTE-V2X Sidelink PC5), the received SINR $\gamma_{ij}^{(r)}$ between transmitting vehicle $i$ and receiving vehicle $j$ over radio technology $r \in \{\text{G5}, \text{LTE}\}$ is expressed as:

$$\gamma_{ij}^{(r)} = \frac{P_{tx}^{(r)} \cdot G_{tx}^{(r)} \cdot G_{rx}^{(r)} \cdot h_{ij}^{(r)} \cdot d_{ij}^{-\alpha_r}}{\sum_{k \neq i} P_{tx, k}^{(r)} \cdot h_{kj}^{(r)} \cdot d_{kj}^{-\alpha_r} + N_0^{(r)} \cdot B^{(r)}}$$
Where $P_{tx}^{(r)}$ is transmit power, $G_{tx}, G_{rx}$ are antenna gains, $h_{ij}$ is the small-scale fading coefficient (Nakagami-$m$ or Rayleigh), $d_{ij}$ is Euclidean distance with path-loss exponent $\alpha_r$, $B^{(r)}$ is channel bandwidth (10/20 MHz), and $N_0^{(r)}$ is thermal noise power spectral density.

B. Optical Wireless Communications (OWC / VLC) Lambertian Model

Sto-CAV includes high-speed Optical Wireless Communication (VLC using vehicular headlights and taillights). The DC channel gain $H_{VLC}(0)$ following a generalized Lambertian radiation pattern is governed by:

$$H_{VLC}(0) = \begin{cases} \frac{(m_L + 1) A_{rx}}{2\pi d_{ij}^2} \cos^{m_L}(\phi_{ij}) T_s(\psi_{ij}) g(\psi_{ij}) \cos(\psi_{ij}), & 0 \le \psi_{ij} \le \Psi_c \\ 0, & \psi_{ij} > \Psi_c \end{cases}$$
Where $m_L = -\frac{\ln(2)}{\ln(\cos(\Phi_{1/2}))}$ is the Lambertian emission order, $A_{rx}$ is active photo-detector area, $\phi_{ij}$ is radiance angle, $\psi_{ij}$ is incidence angle, $T_s(\psi)$ is optical filter transmission, $g(\psi) = \frac{n^2}{\sin^2(\Psi_c)}$ is optical concentrator gain with refractive index $n$, and $\Psi_c$ is receiver Field-of-View (FoV) semi-angle.

C. End-to-End Latency Decomposition

Sto-CAV models total transaction latency $\tau_{e2e}$ by aggregating all modular processing and transmission delays:

$$\tau_{e2e} = \tau_{gen} + \tau_{queue} + \tau_{tx}^{(r)} + \tau_{prop} + \tau_{proc} + \tau_{DIL}$$
Where $\tau_{DIL}$ represents the physical round-trip hardware serialization and socket latency across geographically distributed testbed nodes.

Modular Breakdown:

  1. Control Unit (CU): Acts as the central orchestrator, managing global simulation clock discrete steps ($\Delta t = 100\text{ ms}$ or $10\text{ ms}$), dispatching kinematics to communication models, and collecting reception statistics.
  2. Traffic Engine Interface: Interacts with SUMO via the TraCI protocol, extracting instantaneous vehicle positions $(x, y)$, velocities $v_i$, and headings $\theta_i$.
  3. Multi-RAT Network Module: Dynamically calculates packet loss, fading, and interference based on transmitter-receiver distance and line-of-sight conditions.
  4. Distributed DIL Bridges: Bridges real physical transceiver hardware (e.g., optical transceivers in Florence and RF OBUs in Bologna) through low-latency ZeroMQ/TCP socket tunnels.
  5. Web Control Panel: Provides researchers with a graphical configuration dashboard to parameterize traffic flow, communication technologies, and retrieve real-time latency CDF curves.

Key Empirical Finding: The experiments demonstrated that while VLC offers unmatched ultra-low latency ($< 2\text{ ms}$) and zero RF congestion for short-range platoon car-following, its performance drops sharply during non-line-of-sight (NLoS) occlusions. Therefore, a hybrid multi-RAT switching strategy (seamlessly failing over to ITS-G5/LTE-V2X) is essential to preserve safety continuity.

6. Critical Limitations of Sto-CAV & The Novel Research Wedge

Identified Limitations in the Base Paper (Sto-CAV):

  1. Zero Cybersecurity & Trust Mechanisms: Sto-CAV assumes an entirely cooperative, honest environment. It lacks any misbehavior detection system (MDS), zero-trust verification engine, or defenses against Byzantine spoofing attacks.
  2. Unchecked Platoon Control Stability: While Sto-CAV measures message latency, it does not evaluate how communication delay and packet tampering degrade physical CACC platoon string stability or cause emergency phantom jams.
  3. Static RAT Selection: Multi-RAT handovers are handled via pre-configured rules rather than dynamic, trust- and deadline-aware adaptive routing.
